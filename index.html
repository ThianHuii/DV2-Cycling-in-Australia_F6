What the comparisons tell us

A national minority.
A local opportunity.

  • Cycling remains uncommon nationally, but local participation differs sharply.
  • Yarra’s higher share shows why counts alone can mislead.
  • Men represent 72% of bicycle commuters in these records.
  • Both sexes record their highest counts at ages 25–34.
  • For Malaysia, compare neighbourhood participation before choosing cycling priorities.
  • Australian evidence alone cannot establish which Malaysian policies will succeed.

Measures, methods and evidence

Read the figures in context

Different measures

The Australian Bureau of Statistics is abbreviated ABS.

The Census surveys Australia’s population every five years.

Cycling shares divide bicycle commuters by all employed people.

Figure 1 instead includes only specified travelling workers.

Counts, shares and recorded locations describe different things.

The 2021 comparison reflects pandemic-era travel conditions.

Geographic names

Local government areas, or LGAs, represent local councils.

Their populations and geographic sizes differ considerably.

NSW: New South Wales; VIC: Victoria; QLD: Queensland.

SA: South Australia; WA: Western Australia; TAS: Tasmania.

NT: Northern Territory; ACT: Australian Capital Territory.

Moreland uses the supplied dataset’s 2021 council name.

Prepared data

Charts load supplied CSVs or reproducible spreadsheet exports.

Figure 8 combines nearby locations using recorded point counts.

Those locations come from participating cyclists’ satellite-position records.

They reveal sampled activity, rather than complete cycling routes.

Figure 10 compares months using each location’s own average.

Historical extracts do not describe today’s cycling levels.
